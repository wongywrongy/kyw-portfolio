## Nebius x NVIDIA Builders & Brews

> "If you want to improve, be content to be thought foolish and stupid." - Epictetus

![](/images/nebius-2.jpg)

I attended the Builders & Brews event for the Nebius x NVIDIA Global AI Hackathon. This was their SF stop on a global tour. I got to talk with a few engineers, but most of the people I met were founders. I felt really out of my comfort zone, given how everyone around me talked with such impact on the tech industry and its future.

It was a small session, but I got a lot out of it between the sponsor credits, networking, and most importantly, knowledge. The merch from both Nebius and Tavily was a plus. I got to meet unique people who were really passionate in this particular space.

Hearing how the Nebius and Tavily teams were thinking about their future, straight from the team, was worth the trip on its own.

## The AI-Native Talent Hunt

![](/images/ainative-2.jpg)

The Emergences Labs x TinyFish AI-Native Talent Hunt was my close to Tech Week, and it was the most intense yet interesting day of my week. Partner companies posted challenges tied to real hiring criteria. They judged the solution, agentic workflow, engineering practices, and prompting behind it.

I entered the Engineering track and finished three challenges:

- **Cresta (Senior ML Engineer)**, top 20%: an AI workflow that applies the right company policy to billing disputes and doesn't guess when evidence is missing.
- **Dify (AI Engineer, Agentic Systems)**, top 30%: evaluating whether an AI support assistant is reliable enough to answer customers directly.
- **OpenArt (Software Engineer, Agent)**: a context-selection algorithm that keeps relevant creative decisions without carrying forward outdated or conflicting instructions.

I didn't win, but placing top 20% and top 30% is something I'm proud of. I was also ranked against people going for full-time and senior roles, most of them were older and more experienced than me.

Thought the event was technically plagued, people kept running into problems just using their software. I lost an hour just trying to get into NeoWork platform. Their engineer was trying to figure out the issue alongside me, but I solved it by feeding their permission check a fake signal. 

Even with the hiccups, I liked the format a lot. I think solving a problem tied to a real job says more about how you work than a resume or a technical round. If this became a real hiring pipeline, I'd like it to be widely adopted in some form.

## Fast Forward by Vercel

![](/images/vercel-2.jpg)

Fast Forward was a one-day hackathon from Vercel and Meta, with Shopify and Neon as integration partners. Their tracks covered long-running agents, multimodal apps, and VR. It ran the day before Vercel Ship SF, so it technically landed after Tech Week, but I'll give it a quick mention.

This event was definitely out of my league. The room was full of founders and well-known open source developers.

I chose Meta's VR/AR track. I'd never touched that space before, and getting to build for Meta's upcoming glasses, set to release next year, was exciting.

I knew I wasn't going to win as soon as I submitted. Despite a lackluster performance, the Meta team still gave me feedback and advice on how to build better on their platform in the future. Spending a day on something completely new was a good reminder of how we can always learn more.
