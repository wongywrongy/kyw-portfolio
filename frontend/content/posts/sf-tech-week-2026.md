## Nebius x NVIDIA Builders & Brews

![](/images/nebius-2.jpg)

> "If you want to improve, be content to be thought foolish and stupid." - Epictetus

I attended the Builders & Brews event for the Nebius x NVIDIA Global AI Hackathon. This was their SF stop on their global tour.

I got to talk with a few engineers, but most of the people I met were founders. I felt really out of my comfort zone, given how everyone around me talked about their impact on the tech industry and its future.

It was a small meetup, but I got a lot out of it between the sponsor credits, networking, and most importantly, knowledge. The merch from both Nebius and Tavily was also great. I'm glad I didn't skip an event like this. Even though it was small, I got to meet unique people who are really passionate about this space.

Hearing how Nebius is thinking about its future, straight from the team, was worth the trip on its own.

## The AI-Native Talent Hunt

![](/images/ainative-2.jpg)

The Emergences Labs x TinyFish AI-Native Talent Hunt closed out Tech Week, and it was the most intense yet interesting day of my week. Partner companies posted challenges tied to real hiring criteria. They judged not just the solution, but also the agentic workflow, engineering practices, and prompting behind it.

I entered the Engineering track, worked with OpenAI's Codex, and finished three challenges:

- Cresta (Senior ML Engineer), top 20%: an AI workflow that applies the right company policy to billing disputes and doesn't guess when evidence is missing.
- Dify (AI Engineer, Agentic Systems), top 30%: evaluating whether an AI support assistant is reliable enough to answer customers directly.
- OpenArt (Software Engineer, Agent): a context-selection algorithm that keeps relevant creative decisions without carrying forward outdated or conflicting instructions.

I didn't win, but placing top 20% and top 30% is something I'm proud of. I was also ranked against people going for full-time and senior roles, most of them older and more experienced than me.

However, the platform had technical issues, and people kept running into problems just opening the challenges. I lost an hour just trying to get into the platform. Their engineer was trying to figure out the issue alongside me, but I solved it by feeding their permission check a fake signal. The engineer was impressed, to say the least.

Even with the hiccups, I liked the format a lot. Their team also did their best to support everyone throughout the day. I think solving a problem tied to a real job says more about how you work than a resume or a technical round. If this became a real hiring pipeline, I'd definitely sign up again.

## Fast Forward by Vercel

![](/images/vercel-2.jpg)

Fast Forward was a one-day hackathon from Vercel and Meta, with Shopify and Neon as integration partners. Tracks covered long-running agents, multimodal apps, and VR. It ran the day before Vercel Ship SF, so it technically landed after Tech Week, but it was my finale.

This event was definitely where I felt fully out of my comfort zone. The room was full of founders and well-known open source developers.

I chose Meta's VR/AR track. I'd never touched that space before, and getting to build for Meta's upcoming glasses, set to release next year, was exciting.

I knew I wasn't going to win as soon as I submitted. However, I learned a lot from the Meta team. They were so eager to help me learn from the ground up. Spending a day around that team while building something completely new to me was a good reminder of how much there is left to learn.
