# Cal Hacks 13.0

> "Great things in business are never done by one person. They're done by a team of people." - Steve Jobs

![The Palace of Fine Arts in San Francisco, home of Cal Hacks 13.0.](/images/palace.jpg)

I woke up at 8 a.m. on Friday, picked up my team, and drove to the Palace of Fine Arts in San Francisco. Over the next three days, my team built an AI cybersecurity platform, I won a cybersecurity challenge, we placed 7th in a Scrabble bot tournament, and ended up at a Z Fellows meetup with Cory Levy.

![Hackers working inside the Cal Hacks 13.0 venue.](/images/calhacks.jpg)

The venue was amazing, and there were so many great people. I met students from across the US, plus people  from Canada, Europe, and Asia.

It was also great to see a lot of familiar faces at the world's largest collegiate hackathon.

# Our project

> "In the age of AI, time is the enemy of enterprise cybersecurity; the old assumption that organizations have time to detect and address vulnerabilities is now obsolete." - McKinsey & Company

We've all followed the Hugging Face attack and the major PHP vulnerabilities, which led us to a hypothetical. As agentic swarms get larger and stronger, cyberattacks will only get worse. Major incidents are already more frequent every year, unfortunately making our hypothetical more real everyday. So what if security teams had agentic defenses, actively and swiftly defending a platform.

That became Sudo-KO. In our demo, an attacking swarm targets a fleet of 200 drones while defender agents identify, isolate, and eliminate vulnerabilities. Drones are just one use case. The same idea applies to any deployed system where an attack can spread from server to server or user to user.

We didn't win, but it was my first submission in a cybersecurity related track. Talking with the teams at SpacetimeDB, Armadin, and Bitdeer was a highlight too as they really appreciated the relevance in our submission.

# Scrabble algorithms

SpacetimeDB ran a Scrabble bot competition alongside the main event. There was so much tense comradery and we made friends from other teams just talking about mixed strategies. My team tested four approaches:

- A neural net
- A classical ML hybrid
- A heuristic algorithm
- Linear regression

Linear regression performed best, so we shipped two models, Triplo and Doppio. They placed 7th out of a few hundred in the final tournament. Our result was surprising. Many of the teams near the top trained statistical models on DGX Sparks and GPU clusters, while our best model had only 29 weights with iterative training.

# Z Fellows Meetup

Yet, the best conversation of the weekend wasn't even on the schedule.

While we were working, someone asked me to scan a QR code with details to meet at a creamery near the Palace of Fine Arts. It turned out to be a Z Fellows meetup with Cory Levy.

He led a great dialogue and gave everyone a chance to pitch one on one. I pitched my idea, got great insights, and heard what others were building. On the spot, he also ran a debate on the future of AI with our group of 10 people. It went for over an hour, full of sharp arguments and perspectives I hadn't heard before.

![My teammate Caleb and me at the Z Fellows meetup.](/images/zcombwcaleb.jpg)

Later, my team stopped by the Y Combinator afterparty, but we didn't stay long. My teammates had flights to catch, and I got so much more out of the CalHacks weekend than I had expected.
